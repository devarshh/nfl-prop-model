Week 9 prop projections

Type a sportsbook’s line and its over and under odds under any prop (or a book’s odds on a touchdown or parlay). The page says Bet, Lean or Pass and why; the rules are at the bottom.

Model run Monday, Nov 9 at 6:50 PM. Final injury designations come out Friday and inactive lists about 90 minutes before kickoff. Projections assume flagged players play, so check both before you bet.

How to read this

The light band covers the middle 80% of likely outcomes, the dark band the middle 50%, and the black tick is the median (the big number). The yellow marker is the line you entered; the shaded area to its right is the over.

Past its outermost percentiles the model extends the distribution with smooth tails, so alternate lines far from the median still get a probability (shown up to 98%; treat the far tails as rough). For receptions on a whole-number line, the probability ignores the push. “Fair” is the price at which a bet on that side would break even if the model were exactly right. At −110 you need 52.4% just to break even.

Every number blends two independent methods. The stats model learns from every player-game since 2016. The game simulation plays each game snap by snap 1,000 times, with each team’s real play-calling tendencies, tempo and 4th-down habits, its QB’s sack, scramble and accuracy profile, and each player’s usage, tuned so the average score matches the betting market. In walk-forward backtests the blend beat the stats model alone for every prop type. When you enter a line, both methods’ own probabilities are shown: if they agree on the side, the signal is stronger; if they split, treat it as close to a coin flip.

Touchdowns. “Anytime TD” is the chance the player scores at least one rushing or receiving touchdown (passing touchdowns, kick and punt returns and 2-point conversions don’t count). It blends the stats model, built on each player’s touchdowns and red-zone and goal-line role, his team’s implied points and the opponent, with the share of the 1,000 simulated games in which he scored. 2+ TDs uses the same blend; First TD (the game’s first touchdown) comes from the simulation alone. Enter a sportsbook’s price, as American odds like +150 or decimal odds like 2.50, to see the expected value of a $1 bet. Books build a big margin into touchdown markets, so most prices will show no value; a positive EV means the model’s probability beats the price, not that the bet will win.

Parlays. Built from the standard “X+” milestone bets (like 50+ rushing yards or 5+ receptions) and touchdown bets. Each leg’s chance is the projection on this page; how often legs hit together comes from the 1,000 simulated games, so it keeps how they move together: a quarterback and his receivers rise and fall together, two backs split the carries, a back’s yards and his touchdown go hand in hand. The most likely parlay is the set of legs that hit together most often with every leg capped (at 70%, 80% or 90% on its own), because without a cap it would stack near-certain legs that pay almost nothing. “Multiplying the legs” is what you’d get treating them as unrelated; sportsbooks adjust same-game parlay prices for correlation their own way, so compare their price with the fair odds. Same-game parlays use one leg per player unless you allow two (a receiver’s catches and his yards are nearly the same bet, and books price them that way). Across games it’s one leg per game, and games don’t affect each other. Questionable players, new starting quarterbacks’ passing and players tagged “Snaps ↓” are left out. In a backtest on 406 games from 2025–26, the suggested same-game parlays of 3–5 legs hit within about 2 points of their predicted chance at the 70% and 80% caps; 2-leg ones hit about 4 points less often than predicted, and 90%-cap ones about 5 points more often.

In the backtest the projections tracked sportsbook lines closely. They haven’t yet been tested on enough real lines to show they beat them, so lean on the probability more than the pick.

Weather uses typical conditions for each stadium. Retractable roofs are assumed closed when the schedule doesn’t say. “New starter” marks a QB making his first start for the team this season or after a change. “New team” marks a player who has changed teams since his last game; his projection still leans on his role with the old team.

How Bet / Lean / Pass is decided

For a yardage or receptions prop, enter the line and both sides’ odds (a blank side counts as −110). The page picks the side with the better expected value and then checks:

  1. Edge. The model’s chance on that side minus the chance the price needs to break even (52.4% at −110). Bet needs 5+ points, Lean 2.5+, anything less is a Pass. The cushion is there because the model hasn’t yet been proven against real lines.
  2. Both methods. If the stats model and the game simulation pick different sides it’s a Pass; for a Bet each must clear break-even on its own.
  3. Caution caps a Bet at Lean: the player is Questionable, missed practice or just changed teams (confirm he’s active); his snap share fell 15+ points in his last two games (the “Snaps ↓” tag: in the 2024–26 backtest these players’ results landed outside the model’s middle 80% about 25% of the time instead of 20%; “Snaps ↑” players were projected fine, so that tag is information only); the line sits in the model’s rough tails (beyond its 5th–95th percentiles); or the model gives its side more than 75%, a gap that big usually means news the model hasn’t seen.
  4. Known biases, adjusted for the verdict only (the projections above are unchanged): new starting QBs’ passing is shaded down 20 yards (both methods ran about 21 yards high on new starters in 2025–26), and for goal-line QBs whose stats-model touchdown chance is 10+ points above the simulation’s, the touchdown chance leans 70% toward the stats model (the simulation runs low on them in the backtest).
  5. Touchdowns and parlays are judged on expected value per $1: Bet at +10¢ or more, Lean at +3¢, with the same checks. Two-leg same-game parlays are marked down 4 points, since they hit about that much less often than predicted in the backtest.

“Bet” is not a guarantee: even a real 5-point edge loses about 45% of the time. Keep stakes small and equal, don’t stack bets that move together (a QB’s over and his receiver’s over are close to the same bet), and download what you entered before the games kick off (games disappear from this page once they start) so the weekly review can grade the suggestions: python review_week.py 9 --bets ~/Downloads/week9_bets.csv.
